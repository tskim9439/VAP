========== sot-streaming
 * Root cause in our format: tags are state-carrying and emitted only on change, as in DiarizationLM, JEDIS and VibeVoice. One missed change leaves no later decision point, which explains why 60% of whole-turn errors are streaks. Published systems that avoid streaks decide the speaker per token (t-vector, WEEND, SURT aux branch, Sortformer word-level tags) and confirm after a short delay. In t-vector's ablation 1-2 words was best; 4-8 words lost change points (LibriCSS cpWER 12.4 at 2 words vs 19.7 at 8). Core V3 change: a per-token slot head plus 2-word hysteresis that relabels words without touching the transcript.
 * TAXI is a special case worth testing before any training. The BAS corpus says the dispatcher always speaks German and the client always English, and our cross-language slots are language-fixed. So correct attribution equals per-token LID, and the observed 'words in the right language under the wrong slot' failure is a tag-language binding failure, not an acoustic one. Diagnostic: on V1/V2 TAXI outputs, count words whose language disagrees with their slot. If that covers most of the 8.0/10.2 attribution share, a language-consistency rule or per-token LID head should close much of the gap. Tune it on synthetic EN-DE phone-band dev, never on TAXI.
 * Why our head-guided decoding hurt while others gain from speaker evidence: we hard-inserted tags from a chunk-level head that wobbles near turn changes. Successful systems either inject the evidence as soft input that the model is trained with (Sortformer kernel: AMI cpWER 32.94 to 28.17; SSA; SA-SOT's speaker-embedding fusion) or decide per word with hysteresis while keeping the transcript fixed (t-vector, DiarizationLM's transcript-preserving transfer). Both avoid the insertion side effect.
 * The V2-a L00 tap probably did nothing because it was a residual path with no speaker objective. JEDIS-LLM shows that a training-only word-level speaker decoder on the encoder output matters a lot (AMI WDER 10.87 without vs 6.97 with). SURT and WEEND both found the speaker branch works best from early layers with full left context (SURT WDER 3.99 with infinite context vs 6.12 when trained with randomized limited context), which matches our probe (L00 96% vs last layer 66%).
 * Missed changes are the documented weak point of change-token systems. Google's T-T speaker-change model had recall 55.8 vs precision 78.1, and USM-SCD recall was about 52% on 96 languages. Fixes that worked: an on-policy minimum-risk loss weighting false rejects 10x (recall 55.8 to 65.2), and decode-time scaling of the turn-token posterior (F1 64.6 to 75.3). A slot-tag logit bias tuned on non-TAXI dev windows is a zero-training probe for us.
 * Our 'recover' training was off-policy (synthetic flips with masked labels) and did not help at ck2000. The corrections that work in the literature train on the model's own hypotheses: Google's N-best FA/FR loss and DiarizationLM's hyp2ora pairs (Fisher WDER 5.32 to 2.37). V3 should build its corrective examples from real decodes of our own training dialogues.
 * Re-emitting the tag at every turn start or long pause ('virtual' same-speaker change tokens, arXiv:2606.13095) is the cheapest structural fix: it caps an error at one turn instead of a streak. Evidence on per-word tags is mixed: Sortformer found word-level better than segment-level (AMI 26.71 vs 28.59), JEDIS found it slightly worse (24.08 vs 23.13). So use per-turn or per-pause re-emission, not per-word.
 * VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming uses the same change-only, label-at-head format, with no speaker cache, yet gets 0.4 attribution share on TAXI. The format is not the gap. The main structural differences are a 1.5-2.0 s speaker decision latency vs our 0.32-0.8 s delta, arrival-order (acoustic) labels, and roughly 420k h of streaming pretraining. Moving labels to the tail did not change their cpWER (31.55 vs 31.56). Decision for the user: may speaker labels be finalized 1-2 words after the words themselves (provisional labels), with words still within 0.8 s?
 * Slot memory or speaker caches (Streaming Sortformer AOSC, SURT speaker prefixing, the JEDIS Speaker Prompt Cache) mainly help when sessions exceed the context or windows reset. SURT's full-session AMI cpWER went from about 96-100 to 82-84 with prefixing, and JEDIS long-form CH109 went from 18.58 without cache updates to 18.20 with them. TAXI sessions (72 s or less) fit in context, so memory is not the first lever for TAXI. It becomes relevant for V2's 20-90 s and meeting windows.
 * NVIDIA's 2026 study using a Nemotron backbone (our encoder family) found single-instance serialized multi-speaker ASR well behind diarization-conditioned multi-instance SSA (cpWER 28.66 vs 15.38 with oracle diarization) and more sensitive to diarization errors. Within the one-model constraint, the closest substitute is to feed our own activity head's posteriors back into the LLM as conditioning.
 * Suggested V3 order within the 1 h / 3000-step budget: (0) zero-training checks: the LID-vs-slot audit of V1/V2 TAXI outputs and a slot-tag logit-bias sweep on dev. (1) Run A: mandatory tag re-emission per turn and pause, plus a per-token slot head and a per-token LID head trained with CE, decoded with 2-word hysteresis and transcript-preserving relabel. (2) Run B: add soft injection of activity-head posteriors (Sortformer kernel, head moved before the LLM or lagged one chunk) and a training-only word-level slot decoder on the adapter output. (3) Run C: on-policy correction fine-tuning from the model's own decodes. Evaluate each with the cp/agn/attribution decomposition and the whole-turn/streak breakdown.
 * Caveats: the Sortformer numbers come from the v1 HTML (the current arXiv v3 may differ). SD-CTC and SEAL were checked at abstract level only. Several cited methods were evaluated offline or on simulated LibriSpeech mixes, so expect smaller gains at 80 ms chunks on real telephone speech. TAXI must stay eval-only, including for threshold tuning.

========== diar-asr-joint
 * The literature agrees our gap is a tracking problem, not recognition. Every strong system feeds an explicit, permutation-resolved speaker signal into the decoder: Sortformer kernels (AMI cpWER 32.94->28.17), G-STAR interleaved tracker cues (Fisher 14.23->10.29 from the fusion alone), and SSA/DiCoW conditioning (NVIDIA study avg cpWER about 15-16 vs 28.7 for single-instance tags). V2-a failed because it fed raw L00 features with no speaker objective, so the LLM still had to solve identity tracking itself. V3 should give the LLM resolved slot posteriors, not more raw features.
 * Problem in the current approach (hypothesis to verify): slot semantics are inconsistent. Same-language windows use arrival order, while cross-language windows use language order with a random flip. As a result, the activity head's targets in cross-language windows are effectively language ID, and the slot to speaker binding depends on a prefix that is randomly flipped. Every reference system uses arrival-order indices (Sortformer, AOSC, G-STAR <spk=k>, VibeVoice 'Speaker k', SD-CTC). Proposal: arrival order everywhere, with language as a per-slot attribute the model emits at first emission (e.g., <SPK_A><de>) or as an unordered declared set. Translation direction then follows the emitted language. Cheap diagnostic first: on collapsed TAXI sessions, test whether misplaced words follow the 'first speaker = A' convention or the declared language map.
 * Problem: the activity head is a fixed-slot BCE (Sort-only) on LLM audio positions, the layer region where speaker info is weakest (69%). Its output reached decoding only as a hard constraint (+4 pt insertions). Fixes from the literature: (a) add PIL to make a hybrid Sort+PIL loss, since Sort-only is clearly worse on 2-speaker telephone, CH109 DER 9.08 vs 5.66/6.27. (b) Move the tracker to early encoder layers (L00 96%) with a few transformer layers that attend over the window plus an AOSC-style cache. (c) Inject its soft posteriors into each chunk embedding via a zero-init or identity-init kernel or embedding, trained with noisy or flipped activity (SE-DiCoW: mask augmentation alone 29.1->16.0 tcpWER).
 * Problem: change-only tags are a state machine with no recovery, and tag tokens are rare in CE, which fits our 60% streaks-after-one-missed-change. JEDIS shows change-only still beats per-word tags for cpWER (CH109 19.46 vs 24.55), so keep the format. Make misses rarer and recoverable instead: (1) weight tag-token CE 2x or more (G-STAR) and add a short FR-weighted EMBR fine-tune (Google SCD: FR x10, recall +16.8% rel). (2) Feed tracker posteriors back as input, so a missed change remains visible in the next chunk and the model can re-tag. Our earlier 'recover' training lacked this evidence.
 * Latency budget is not the blocker. Streaming Sortformer with AOSC keeps 2-speaker DER flat from 10 s to 0.32 s latency (CALLHOME 2-spk 6.80 vs 6.86; CH109 5.27 vs 5.41) at our 80 ms frame rate. VibeVoice's attribution relies on 0.5 s lookahead and 2-3 s chunks, and lookahead helps attribution more than WER (L0->L4 avg cpWER 35.88->31.55). Since our LLM emits the words for chunk t-delta at step t, the speaker cue for those words should be computed for t-delta using up to delta chunks of lookahead. Check that the head and injection are time-aligned to the emitted span, not to the newest chunk.
 * Put speaker supervision where speaker info lives. JEDIS: removing the encoder-side speaker loss raises WDER from 6.97 to 10.87, but frame-level alignment labels on the encoder hurt cpWER. SD-CTC learns arrival-order speaker assignment from per-speaker transcripts alone (-26% rel cpWER), and its LDA shows SOT encoders blur speakers, matching our probe. A training-only SD-CTC or word-level speaker decoder on the adapter output is the cheapest V3 component, fits a 1 h run, and needs no new data.
 * Proposed V3 order, each piece in-model and teachers only at training. Step 0, diagnostics, no training: TAXI collapse by convention; activity-head accuracy on TAXI vs tag accuracy (if the head is right while tags are wrong, the injection path is the fix; if the head is also wrong, tracker capacity or data is the fix); errors bucketed by number of prior changes. Step 1, low effort: unify arrival-order slots, hybrid Sort+PIL head, tag-token weighting, SD-CTC/word-level speaker loss on the adapter. Step 2: early-layer tracker with AOSC whose posteriors are kernel-injected into chunk embeddings with noise augmentation, optionally distilled from NVIDIA Streaming Sortformer posteriors (the user must download that checkpoint). Step 3, fallback: per-slot conditioned decoding passes (SSA/DiCoW style) driven by the internal tracker, at about 2x LLM compute.
 * Guard recognition. NVIDIA's controlled study shows single-instance tag models with injected diarization can badly hurt single-speaker WER (OpenASR 7.16->16.95), while conditioned per-speaker decoding preserves it (7.44). Keep single-speaker and phone-band data in the mix, and use zero or identity init for every new injection path. Report agn alongside cp so V3 does not trade recognition for attribution. Also check window cutting for speech spilling across cut points, which mislabels slots (NVIDIA introduced PI-DTW for this).
 * Memory / 'slot memory': TAXI (<=72 s) fits our window, so a cache is not what fixes TAXI. The 20-90 s windows, meetings and real sessions do need one. AOSC (a per-slot buffer of acoustic frames selected by the model's own posteriors, 15 s total) is the lightest proven option and involves no external model. Text+audio prompt caches (JEDIS SPC, Speaker-Reasoner) are heavier, assume chunks of 3 s or more, and SPC uses a d-vector check.
 * Evidence gaps: no paper studies cross-language telephone attribution where slot and language can be confused. The NTT speaker-change-mask paper and BA-SOT were seen only as abstracts. Most LLM-based results (G-STAR, JEDIS, Speaker-Reasoner, TagSpeech) are chunk-wise or offline with 7B backbones, so gains on a 0.6B streaming LLM under 0.8 s must be validated on our dev sets, never on TAXI.

========== llm-speaker
 * In the papers opened, attribution gains came from explicit speaker objectives, not from giving the LLM acoustic features alone. Examples: Granite speaker-cluster tags (Fisher WDER 1.5→1.0, CallHome 3.7→2.5), JEDIS word-level speaker supervision (AMI WDER 10.87→6.97), a length-aware speaker loss (AliMeeting cpCER 28.54→27.16), and our own spk-act head (-4 pt). Our V2-a tap had no speaker loss and left the attribution share unchanged (7.4→7.4). VibeVoice has no acoustic-tokenizer ablation. SpeakerSleuth (ACL 2026) reports that audio LLMs favour text over acoustics. V3 should therefore pair the low-layer tap with a speaker-discriminative objective: cluster-ID tag tokens, a training-only slot-sequence decoder, or token-level embedding distillation, with teachers used only in training.
 * Our error profile (73% whole-turn errors, 60% of them streaks after one missed change) follows directly from change-only, state-carrying tags. The literature offers three fixes. (1) Recall-oriented loss on tag tokens: Google raised speaker-change recall from 55.8 to 65.2 at equal precision; SA-DiCoW uses 5× CE on speaker tokens; length-aware weighting is a third option. (2) Periodic re-anchoring of slot identity (SyncLLM re-declares speakers every chunk). (3) Per-slot conditioned decoding (SSA). Evidence on per-word tags is mixed: Sortformer found word-level better (AMI 26.71 vs 28.59), JEDIS found it worse (24.08 vs 23.13). Coarse re-anchoring at turn resumption or every ~1 s is the safer middle ground.
 * Review of the existing approach (hypothesis behind the TAXI collapse): we use two slot conventions, arrival order in same-language windows and language-name order with a random flip in cross-language windows. Every system found (Sortformer, VibeVoice, Granite, JEDIS, MLC-SLM systems) uses one first-appearance convention. V2 shifted the mix toward same-language, meeting and 3-4-speaker windows, which plausibly strengthened the 'first speaker → slot A' prior over the prefix's language mapping. Zero-cost check: in the 5 collapsed TAXI sessions, is the first speaker's language the slot-B language? If yes, either unify to arrival-order slots with a per-turn language token, or rebalance cross-language windows so the B-language speaker often talks first.
 * The VibeVoice comparison is not like-for-like. Its streaming attribution comes at 1.53-2.0 s expected latency (vs our 0.8 s), with a 7B LLM and ~420k h of streaming pretraining. Its own ablations show lookahead 0→0.5 s changes cpWER by 4.3 pt, chunk 2.0→2.9 s by 4.1 pt, and 1.5B→7B by 12.8 pt. The 'second acoustic encoder' explanation is not established, and concat-then-linear fusion equals summed connectors, so the fusion style is not the difference either.
 * On the same Nemotron streaming encoder, NVIDIA's comparison shows per-speaker conditioned decoding (SSA) far ahead of single-instance speaker-token SOT: average cpWER 15.91-23.36 vs 33.46 with predicted diarization. Single-instance SOT also degraded single-speaker WER (7.16→16.95). If the cheap fixes plateau, the structural V3-b is per-slot decoding conditioned on the model's own activity head. It stays one network but needs two decodes, and it removes tag decisions entirely, so a language-slot collapse becomes impossible.
 * Data: synthetic conversations built by concatenating speaker turns consistently help attribution. Granite cut WDER 26-38% with FisherA/MLS concatenations. SpeakerLM needed ~5k h simulated plus 7.6k h real to beat cascades (212 h was not enough). VibeVoice used 4.5k h of TTS meetings. 'Mind the Gap' (Interspeech 2026, abstract only) reports that more overlap helps ASR but hurts diarization, and that source diversity beats domain match. Since V2's meetings and 3-4-speaker data hurt TAXI, V3 should add phone-band, low-overlap, cross-language 2-speaker synthetic data, for example from CallHome German speaker sides plus English, instead of more meetings.
 * Layer choice for the tap: Granite's sweep chose encoder layer 3 of 16, concatenated with the final output; combining several layers did not help. Our L00 was chosen by cosine separability, which may not match what the LLM can use. Sweep L01-L04 with concat or a non-zero-init connector, and judge it together with an attached speaker loss.
 * Suggested V3 order, cheapest first, each a ~1 h run on 2x8 H200: (a) TAXI slot-convention diagnostic, no training; (b) tag-token CE upweighting plus length-aware weighting; (c) speaker-cluster ID tokens in the tags (k-means K≈100 over ECAPA embeddings of training speakers, teacher used offline only); (d) a training-only slot-sequence decoder or CTC on the tap/adapter output, replacing or adding to the frame-level BCE head; (e) target-slot instruction tasks in the MT-LLM style; (f) coarse tag re-anchoring; (g) per-slot conditioned decoding. Steps b-e keep a single model, add no inference latency, and fit the ≤0.8 s budget.
 * Verification caveats: SEAL, BA-SOT, base DiCoW (2501.00114), the multitalker-parakeet model card (fetch failed), and the full text of 'Mind the Gap' were not opened, so their details come from search snippets. For t-SOT/t-vector only the abstract was seen, so no numbers are reported. No files were modified.

========== speaker-repr
 * Every strong 2025-26 system I opened labels speakers by order of first appearance: Sortformer, Streaming Sortformer with its arrival-order speaker cache (AOSC), G-STAR, VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming and JEDIS-LLM. Our cross-language windows use a different rule, slot = language (fixed or randomly flipped), while same-language windows use arrival order. The model therefore has to learn two binding rules. The TAXI failure (both speakers' words in one language slot) is consistent with a missed or broken binding. For V3, use arrival order in every window, and declare languages as a set or let the model emit a language token after each slot tag. cpWER is permutation-invariant, so naming does not affect the score.
 * Our dominant error (73% whole-turn, 60% of those streaks after one missed change) is the known weak point of change detection built into ASR: baseline recall is about 50-62% on Callhome and Fisher (Google, ICASSP 2023). Every source that fixed it changed the loss to emphasise speaker tokens. Google weighted missed and spurious changes about 10x in an N-best loss (Callhome recall 50.6 → 59.1). G-STAR weights speaker-token CE 2x. Zheng et al. (Interspeech 2026) weight speaker-ID CE by segment length. Our tags are sparse and emitted only on change, so they get almost no gradient. This is the cheapest high-value change.
 * Bound streak length by design: re-emit the current slot tag at every turn start (after <EOT>), and optionally after long pauses, instead of only on change (cf. virtual <SC> tokens in 2606.13095). A missed change then propagates at most to the next turn. The evidence on granularity is mixed: JEDIS found per-segment tags better than per-word tags, while Sortformer v1 found word-level speaker tokens best. So re-assert per turn, not per word.
 * Injecting speaker information only helps in the literature when the injected stream is itself trained on a speaker objective: Sortformer posteriors (AMI cpWER 32.94 → 28.17), tracker embeddings in G-STAR (Fisher 14.23 → 10.29), CAM++ embeddings (2606.13095), t-vectors. Our V2-a fed raw L00 features through a zero-initialised adapter and left the 2-speaker attribution share unchanged (7.4). The fix is to supervise the tap branch on speaker targets: teacher distillation from ECAPA, CAM++ or WavLM-SV at training only, or a supervised contrastive/AAM loss with hard negatives. Then feed the LLM derived cues (activity posteriors, similarity to each slot's prototype) in their own interleaved positions; interleaving beat feature concatenation by 1.7-2.5 cpCER.
 * Head-guided decoding hurt because it imposes a hard rule. The literature uses soft conditioning learned end-to-end: Sortformer kernels, or a gated FFN of the form X + FFN(X ⊙ p). Our activity head was the only big win (−4 cpWER). Feed its posteriors back as features, and keep the head anchored by its own loss, possibly with a stop-gradient from the LLM loss: joint fine-tuning erased Sortformer's ASR gain (32.74 vs 28.17). Train on a mix of oracle and predicted activity, since the published oracle-vs-predicted results go both ways.
 * The 'slot memory' the user hesitated about is now a standard, validated streaming component. AOSC holds about 15 s of frames chosen by posterior score, is ordered by arrival and has no permutation step. It loses little in streaming: CH109 DER is 5.37 offline vs 6.60 at 0.32 s latency. For us the cheapest form is per-slot prototypes built from tap features, plus a cosine-similarity cue injected at each 80 ms step. For TAXI-length sessions (72 s or less), memory capacity is not the bottleneck because the LLM context already holds the history. The value is an explicit, speaker-discriminative 'A vs B' comparison at each step.
 * Latency is part of the competitor gap. VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming decides speakers with 1.5-2.0 s latency (2-2.9 s chunks plus 0.5 s lookahead), and its lookahead ablation alone moves mean cpWER from 35.88 to 31.55. The t-vector paper shows speaker-ID error falling with decision delay (SER 3.8% at 0.68 s vs 1.2% at 2.72 s) but more missed changes on long-form audio. With δ ≤ 0.8 s we need stronger cues per decision rather than expecting parity from data alone. Use δ10 as the attribution operating point.
 * Exposure bias likely drives the streaks: training uses a correct tag history, inference uses the model's own. Randomly flipped spans ('recover') did not mimic real errors. A better V3 option: replay the previous checkpoint's real tag errors as history and supervise recovery at the next turn, and/or apply DPO on its collapse windows (2603.24034: hypothesis history + 0.5 dropout + DPO took TED-LIUM3 WER from 5.59 to 5.17; without dropout it got worse). I found no published scheduled-sampling result for speaker tags, so treat this as moderately supported.
 * Data audit (hypotheses, unverified on our side). (a) Synthetic cross-language windows built from different corpora may let the model use corpus or channel differences as the speaker cue; TAXI is a single mono phone mix with no such cue. Apply channel augmentation to the whole mix. (b) V2 added meetings, 3-4-speaker synthetic data and longer windows while keeping cross-language data at 2 speakers, which may have diluted the telephone-like cross-language condition. Check the mixing ratio. (c) Simulated conversations built from real turn statistics beat simulated mixtures (Callhome DER 8.64 vs 15.09 without fine-tuning). CallHome German speaker segments (all 18.4 h) can supply those statistics and attribution-only losses even where transcripts are masked.
 * Suggested V3 order within roughly 1 h runs, each judged on the 2-speaker attribution share, synthetic EN-DE dev and CallHome German, with TAXI kept strictly held-out. Run 1 (cheap): arrival-order slots everywhere, tag re-assertion per turn, tag CE up-weighting or length-aware weighting, explicit speaker-change head, Sort+PIL hybrid activity loss. Run 2: a supervised speaker branch on L00 (teacher distillation plus contrastive with hard negatives), activity-posterior kernels and slot-prototype similarity cues into the LLM input, plus a word-level slot-ID auxiliary. Run 3: on-policy history and DPO for collapse cases. Data fixes run alongside (whole-mix channel augmentation, VTLP speaker diversity, similar-voice pairs).
 * Evidence gaps to state honestly. I found no attribution or cpWER evidence for voice-conversion augmentation in conversation simulation, none for narrowband-specific speaker-feature robustness beyond real-telephone fine-tuning and mixed-bandwidth training anecdotes, and none for scheduled sampling of speaker tags. DiarizationLM (Interspeech 2024; −55.5% WDER on Fisher, −44.9% on Callhome English after fine-tuning) is a second-pass LLM corrector. It conflicts with streaming and the single-pass constraint, so it is listed only for context.

========== telephone-xling
 * TAXI is a role=language corpus: per BAS, the dispatcher always speaks German and the client always English (94 dialogues over public network and GSM lines). Our 8.0 (V1) and 10.2 (V2) pt attribution shares on TAXI are therefore entirely a slot-language binding failure, not missing acoustic speaker information. The words are transcribed in the right language but tagged into the wrong slot. Cheapest first step, with no training: re-slot each hypothesis turn of the existing V1/V2 TAXI outputs by text language, rescore, and measure how much of the share disappears. Do not tune anything on TAXI.
 * Problem in the current design: <SPK_A>/<SPK_B> mean two incompatible things. In same-language windows they mean arrival order; in cross-language windows they mean language order with a random flip. V2 shifted the mix toward arrival-order data (cross-language synthetic is ~16% of steps; meetings and 3-4 speaker windows were added). V2 also trained that declared slots may stay empty (half the windows declare 4 slots). Both plausibly explain why V2 collapses two speakers into one language slot more often than V1, consistent with the observations that C/D tokens are 0 and that turning the tap off makes it worse. Check in the collapsed sessions whether the first speaker's language differs from the slot-A language. V3 fix: separate, language-keyed slot tokens (or a per-turn language token that determines the slot deterministically) in cross-language mode, and never let a declared cross-language slot be empty in training.
 * Problem in the current design: speaker tags are emitted before the words, and only on change. That forces an irrevocable slot decision at turn onset, when only ~0.3-0.8 s of audio (delta 4-10) is available, and an error persists until the next detected change. Shafey et al. 2019 report the same pattern: 'once roles were assigned incorrectly they stayed flipped'. Remedies that fit streaming: re-assert the slot tag (optionally with a voice-cluster or language token) at every turn start after <EOT>; upweight false-reject cost on change decisions (Google FA/FR loss: recall 55.8 -> 65.2); and train correction on the model's own mistakes (on-policy, via DiarizationLM's TPST/hyp2ora) rather than random flips, which had no effect. JEDIS found per-word speaker IDs hurt cpWER, so re-assert per turn, not per word.
 * Problem in the current design: V2-a added a path from L00 (96% separable) into the LLM input but no objective that needs it, and the 2-speaker attribution share stayed at 7.4. Published systems that make intermediate-layer speaker information useful pair it with pressure: IBM Granite concatenates layer 3 and trains on Fisher with speaker-cluster tags (CallHome WDER 3.7 -> 2.5); JEDIS-LLM adds a training-only speaker decoder (CH109 cpWER 19.5); SD-CTC adds per-slot CTC (2-mix cpWER 4.7 -> 3.5). Add one of these losses on the adapter+tap output, and re-run the M0 probe on the LLM input to confirm the separability actually rises.
 * Our only large win was a frame-level speaker-activity loss (-4 cpWER); hard-forcing tags from it hurt (+4 pt insertions). The literature's middle path is soft injection: Sortformer and NVIDIA SSA add predicted per-speaker activity to the recognizer input through sinusoidal kernels or a small injection MLP (CH109 cpWER 24.80 -> 22.22 / 21.45). An internal causal activity branch on lower encoder layers, injected softly into the adapter output, keeps one model, adds no latency, and lets the LLM learn when to trust it. Streaming Sortformer's arrival-order speaker cache (0.32 s latency, CALLHOME 2-spk DER 8.6%) is a proven template if the 'slot memory' idea is revisited, mainly for long sessions rather than TAXI.
 * Data matters more than architecture at our scale. Every strong 2-speaker telephone attribution result trained on Fisher (~1.9k h): DiarizationLM, Granite, JEDIS, VibeVoice. SpeakerLM's end-to-end model trails cascades at ~212 h of speaker-labelled conversation and wins only at ~7.6k h. At MLC-SLM 2025 the only end-to-end LLM ranked far below cascades (27.25 vs 16.53 tcpMER). Most valuable external downloads, all needing the user: Fisher English, and the MLC-SLM train set (1,507 h of 2-speaker phone-recorded conversations including English, German and Korean). Under the one-model rule, teachers (ECAPA clusters, WeSpeaker/HDBSCAN pseudo-labels, an activity teacher) can inject cascade knowledge at training time only.
 * Telephone realism, a gap found in the code: vapasr/data/phone_aug.py is applied once to the mixed window (cst_dialogue.py line 713), so both speakers share an identical channel. In real mono-summed calls each side has its own line, handset and codec (TAXI mixes network and GSM). Applying independent per-speaker phone chains before mixing, randomized so it does not become a shortcut, should teach channel as a speaker cue. Combine this with turn-taking simulation fitted on real dialogues: Landini CH2 DER 15.09 -> 8.64; Mind the Gap synthetic+real tcpWER 10.9 -> 8.8. Note that boosting overlap helped recognition but hurt diarization, and short backchannels in the listener's language are a likely attribution error source to include in synthesis.
 * Cross-language conversation literature is thin. STAC (EMNLP 2023) does single-channel conversational ST on mono-summed Fisher-CALLHOME with [TURN]/[XT] tokens, and its speaker-change F1 matches pyannote (77.6 vs 75.8 Fisher), but it does not track identity. MUSCAT (2026) has bilingual one-language-per-speaker discussions but evaluates only WER, and it reports models translating into the previous language or failing at switches. I found no paper that uses language identity as a speaker-attribution constraint in an end-to-end model, so language-keyed slots would be a novel and paper-worthy CST contribution. Token-level language diarization (Liu et al.) shows that incorporating a language signal works better than disentangling it.
 * Suggested V3 order, cheapest and highest-expected-value first, each as a single change against the V2 or V1 control at the same seed and step: (1) TAXI re-slot-by-language diagnostic plus a check of the arrival-order leak in the collapsed sessions. (2) Language-keyed cross-language slots with per-turn tag re-assertion, and no empty declared slots in cross-language windows. (3) Upweighted tag and change loss. (4) Per-speaker phone channels plus a fitted turn-taking simulator. (5) SD-CTC or an aux speaker decoder on adapter+tap, or voice-cluster tags from offline ECAPA k-means. (6) Internal activity branch with soft kernel injection. (7) Slot cache memory only if long-session linking remains a problem.
 * Metrics: compute the speaker-agnostic baseline as MeetEval ORC-WER (or DI-cpWER) so the attribution share = cpWER - ORC-WER is standard. Add tcpWER (5 s collar, as in MLC-SLM and VibeVoice) and WDER (as in the Fisher/CallHome literature, where strong systems reach 1-2.5% WDER on Fisher/CallHome; CH109 cpWER is ~18-22 for strong LLM systems). Add a slot-language purity metric to detect collapse, and use session-level bootstrap, since most TAXI regressions concentrate in a handful of short sessions.
 * Unverified or unopened, so not used for numbers: Kanda et al. 2022 token-level speaker embeddings for streaming t-SOT (arXiv 2203.16685; the HTML render failed); DISPLACE 2023/2024 language-plus-speaker diarization (only abstracts and one team report seen; no evidence there that language cues were used for speaker attribution); NeMo multitalker-parakeet model card details beyond the SSA paper.
