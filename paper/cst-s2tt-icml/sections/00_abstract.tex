\begin{abstract}
Simultaneous speech translation is mostly built and evaluated for one speaker translated in one fixed direction.
Machine interpreting between two people who share no language breaks both assumptions: one device hears both speakers in a single mono stream, and the system must decide in real time who is speaking, which way to translate, and when to commit text it can never retract.
We formalise this task as \taskfull (\task) and introduce \bench, which renders two-language dialogues as continuous single-channel sessions with natural gaps, translation-mediated gaps or turn-end overlap; it pairs German--English telephone dialogues with real speech and synthetic timing (86 sessions, 640 turns with human translations), released as deterministic build scripts and checksums, with an evaluation-only synthetic track for English--German and Korean--English.
Its session-level scorer reports per-direction translation quality, lag within a turn, the listener's wait after each turn, switch latency and wrong-direction output, plus speaker-attributed transcription and diarization errors; controlled perturbations test each metric, and Kendall's $\tau$ between system rankings on synthetic and real sessions is \tbdtext{}.
With realistic instead of oracle segmentation, the best open pipeline loses \tbdtext{} COMET and emits \tbdtext{}\% wrong-direction words under turn-end overlap, and segmentation and routing account for \tbdtext{} and \tbdtext{} COMET points of this loss.
We also introduce \model, an open streaming speech LLM that writes speaker-tagged transcripts and direction-tagged translations into one token stream on an 80\,ms clock, with translation commits learned from alignment-verified meaning units; without oracle input, it recovers \tbdtext{}\% of the gap between oracle and realistic segmentation at a median wait of \tbdtext{}\,s after each turn.
