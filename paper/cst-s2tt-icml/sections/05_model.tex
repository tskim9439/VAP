We cast \task as serialized multi-channel emission on one clock (\cref{fig:model}): after each 80\,ms audio embedding, a causal speech LLM writes zero or more tokens for a speaker-attributed transcript and for a translation into each language.
Each channel trails its evidence by its own delay, so the read/write policy becomes next-token prediction against targets that, relative to a teacher's alignment, do not anticipate unheard speech.
These \emph{conversational delayed streams} extend delayed streams modeling \citep{zeghidour2025streaming}, the per-word delayed translation targets of Hibiki \citep{labiausse2025highfidelity} and serialized output training \citep{kanda2022streaming,papi2023token} to channels with their own delays and commit points.

\subsection{Streaming backbone}\label{sec:model-backbone}
The encoder, Nemotron~3.5 ASR Streaming 0.6B \citep{nvidia2026nemotron35asr}, is a cache-aware \citep{noroozi2024stateful} FastConformer \citep{gulati2020conformer,rekesh2023fast} with 24 layers, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=1024$ and 80\,ms frames; its attention context $[56,0]$ spans 4.48\,s to the left and none to the right, and a truncation test bounds its effective look-ahead by 80\,ms.
An MLP adapter (LayerNorm, Linear, GELU, Linear) maps frame $k$ to an embedding $a_k$ that replaces an audio placeholder token of the Qwen3-ASR language model, the \emph{thinker} \citep{shi2026qwen3asr,xu2025qwen3omni}; we drop Qwen3-ASR's own non-causal audio encoder and use the 1.7B thinker (a 0.6B one also exists).
After a prefix with the language and a delay token \mdltok{DELAY\_\delta}, each chunk holds $a_k$, the text tokens due in it, and \mdltok{next} (``read 80\,ms more''), the analogue of a transducer blank \citep{graves2012sequence,seide2024speech}.
A word that ends at time $t$ (by forced alignment) is due, with all its BPE tokens, in chunk
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delay}
  k(t;\delta)=\lfloor t/\Delta\rfloor+\delta,\qquad \Delta=80\,\mathrm{ms},
\end{equation}
with $\delta\in\{2,3,4,6,8\}$ sampled per sequence (\cref{fig:delta-delay}).
The labels, at positions $\mathcal{Y}$ of the token sequence $o$, are the text tokens and \mdltok{next}; as \mdltok{next} makes up 70--85\% of them, the loss is a weighted cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
  \mathcal{L}=-\frac{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Y}}w_m\log p_\theta(o_m\mid o_{<m})}{\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Y}}w_m},
\end{equation}
with $w_m=1$ for text and $w_m=0.3$ (English) or $0.15$ (Korean) for \mdltok{next}; a final (non-streaming) mode is trained jointly.

In earlier backbones with the 0.6B thinker, the delay token set the median emission delay $(k+1)\Delta-t$ (without encoder look-ahead) to about 200/280/360\,ms for $\delta=2/3/4$; after one epoch, the 1.7B backbone reaches an English VoxPopuli-AA WER of 7.70/7.18/6.84\% at $\delta=4$/$\delta=6$/final, against 6.88\% for the same encoder with its causal RNN-T decoder and 5.10\% for the non-causal offline Qwen3-ASR-1.7B (\cref{tab:backbone}).
A $[56,3]$ right context and a larger $\delta$ are largely interchangeable at matched mean delay (\cref{fig:backbone}), so the encoder stays causal. % optional
All of this is single-speaker English and Korean transcription (\cref{app:model}).

\subsection{One serialized stream}\label{sec:model-stream}
\model keeps this clock (\cref{fig:model}c); its prefix names the language pair and two delays, \mdltok{DELAY\_\delta_s} for transcripts and \mdltok{TDELAY\_\delta_t} for translations, sampled per training session.
Chunk $k$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chunk}
\begin{aligned}
  B_k = a_k\;&\mdltok{\hat s_1}\,z_1\,\mdltok{\hat s_2}\,z_2\cdots\\
             &\mdltok{{\to}\lambda_1}\,y_1\,\mdltok{{\to}\lambda_2}\,y_2\cdots\;\mdltok{next},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $z_j$ are words of speaker $\hat s_j\in\{\mathrm{A},\mathrm{B}\}$, numbered by arrival without enrolment, and $y_i$ translates one unit into language $\lambda_i$, which fixes the direction; either part may be empty.
Each tagged segment is one committed piece (\cref{sec:task}), so the latent speaker and direction state is explicit.
Under turn-end overlap, the words of both speakers are ordered by end time, as in t-SOT \citep{kanda2022streaming} and in JSTAR's speaker-labelled targets \citep{moritz2025transcribing}.
Tags count as text in \cref{eq:loss}; a two-slot activity head on the audio positions adds a binary cross-entropy and serves DER, with arrival-order slots \citep{park2025sortformer} instead of permutation-free training \citep{fujita2019end}.
The KV cache holds the whole session, so both sides of the dialogue condition every decision.
The longest TAXI session (90.02\,s) spans about 1.1k audio positions. % optional

\subsection{Commit targets}\label{sec:model-targets}
Transcript words are force-aligned per speaker before mixing (Qwen3-ForcedAligner; \citealp{shi2026qwen3asr}) and placed by \cref{eq:delay} with $\delta=\delta_s$.
For translations, a teacher LLM (Qwen3.8-27B) translates the source turn $u_{1:I}$ into $v$, and contextual alignment \citep{labiausse2025highfidelity} maps target word $v_j$ to $\alpha_j=\arg\max_i\,[\phi_j(i)-\phi_j(i-1)]$, where $\phi_j(i)$ is the log-probability of $v_j$ when $v$ is force-decoded from $u_{1:i}$.
Source position $i$ is a candidate if the words with $\alpha_j\le i$ form a prefix of $v$ longer than the committed one (the alignment analogue of meaning units; \citealp{zhang2020learning}) and if the source has grown by a minimum unit since the last commit (pilot: 2 Korean or 3 English words, and 1.0\,s).
The teacher then translates only the new part, given the source so far and the committed text; the unit is accepted if this text is in the target language and reaches a chrF of at least 50 against the newly aligned span of $v$.
We call accepted units verified meaning units (MU2).
A turn end flushes the rest of the turn, so the model must learn to recognise turn ends.
A unit whose source ends at time $e$ is written in chunk $\lfloor e/\Delta\rfloor+\delta_t$, with $\delta_t\ge\delta_s$ so that its transcript comes first.
Relative to the teacher's alignment, no target thus anticipates unheard speech, as with Hibiki's per-word delays, whereas sentence-level targets used with prefix-to-prefix training \citep{ma2019stacl} demand anticipating German clause-final verbs or Korean predicates.
The commit points, however, are chosen with the whole turn in view, so \model imitates a privileged teacher, which \cref{tab:main} scores on gold transcripts as Gold$\to$LLM (MU2).
In a text-level \pair{Ko}{En} pilot with silver references (\cref{app:units}), MU2 roughly halves computation-unaware LAAL \citep{papi2022overgeneration}, here normalised by the output length, relative to commits at the utterance end (11.09$\to$5.19\,s \koen; 15.05$\to$7.33\,s \enko), while chrF falls from 61.3 to 60.2 and from 44.2 to 42.0.
Utterance-end LAAL equals the mean utterance length, whereas \taxi turns average 3.086\,s (German) and 4.624\,s (English), which bounds what commits inside a turn can gain there (\cref{fig:turn-length}).
Unverified alignment-only units, continued from a forced prefix, collapsed in 31 of 150 \enko outputs, MU2 in one. % optional

\subsection{Training without real cross-lingual conversations}\label{sec:model-training}
As TAXI is test-only and we know of no two-language conversations with overlap and translations at training scale, \model learns from single-speaker corpora and simulated sessions in stages (\cref{tab:training}).
After an adapter-only warm-up (Stage~0), Stage~1 trained the backbone on about 3.8k\,h + 4.3k\,h of English and Korean speech. % optional
Stage~2 trains M1, which transcribes and attributes speakers: it adds German ASR data, 8\,kHz telephone-band augmentation, speaker tags and the activity head on simulated two-speaker \pair{En}{De} and \pair{Ko}{En} mono sessions with gaps and overlaps drawn more broadly than in the benchmark \citep[cf.][]{landini2022from}.
Stage~3 trains M2 and M3, which add translation streams committed at turn ends (M2) or at MU2 (M3), from human \pair{En}{De} speech translation data \citep{wang2021covost,digangi2019must} and silver teacher translations.
Unlike the off-the-shelf baselines, \model thus sees benchmark-like simulated sessions, as does the same-backbone cascade through M1 (\cref{sec:exp-ablation}).
The fixed-gap sweep (\cref{fig:gap-sweep}) tests whether \model's gains depend on the benchmark's gaps. % optional
Training excludes TAXI, the dialogue corpora and voices of \syn, and outputs of EXAONE-4.0, an LLM we use only for evaluation (\cref{app:syn,app:units}).

\subsection{Inference and latency accounting}\label{sec:model-inference}
Decoding is greedy, with one forward pass per chunk plus one per emitted token.
A piece written after chunk $k$ is time-stamped $(k+1)\Delta+\ell_{\mathrm{enc}}+c$, so a token emitted on schedule after evidence that ends at $t$ has latency $q+\delta\Delta+\ell_{\mathrm{enc}}+c$: frame quantization $q=(\lfloor t/\Delta\rfloor+1)\Delta-t\in(0,\Delta]$, stream delay $\delta\in\{\delta_s,\delta_t\}$, encoder look-ahead $\ell_{\mathrm{enc}}$ (counted at its 80\,ms bound) and compute time $c$, which the ideal clock sets to zero.
As a turn end forces a commit, a model that follows its targets keeps \eo near $q+\delta_t\Delta+\ell_{\mathrm{enc}}+c$.
Real time is not yet shown on GPUs: on an H200, the 0.6B backbone needs 100--150\,ms per 80\,ms chunk at the 99th percentile, and the 1.7B thinker is unmeasured (\cref{tab:ca-latency}).
