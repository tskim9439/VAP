\paragraph{Simultaneous translation.}
Read/write policies range from wait-$k$ to local agreement and attention-guided emission \citep{ma2019stacl,liu2020lowlatency,papi2023alignatt}, and segmentation is learned inside the model \citep{zhang2023endtoend} or handled for unbounded audio (StreamST; \citealp{papi2024streamatt}).
These methods, like StreamSpeech, SeamlessStreaming and Hibiki(-Zero) \citep{zhang2024streamspeech,seamless2023seamless,labiausse2025highfidelity,labiausse2026simultaneous}, assume one speaker and one direction, as do SimulST toolkits and latency metrics \citep{ma2020simuleval,gaido2025simulstream,polak2025better}; our scorer, \scorer, instead resegments per output language, reports SimulEval's \eo per turn and StartOffset at direction changes (switch latency), and counts wrong-direction words (\cref{sec:bench-scoring}).

\paragraph{Speakers and turns.}
Serialized output training transcribes overlapping speakers in one sequence, also in time order (t-SOT) and with speaker attribution \citep{kanda2020serialized,kanda2022streaming,kanda2022streamingspeaker}.
DiariST extends t-SOT to speaker-attributed streaming translation \citep{yang2024diarist}, and STAC-ST merges the two sides of Fisher--CALLHOME calls into one channel \citep{zuluagagomez2023end}.
Turn-taking models predict both speakers' voice activity but learn from monolingual dyads \citep{ekstedt2022voice,inoue2024multilingual}.

\paragraph{Conversational data.}
Of the corpora in \cref{tab:corpora}, only TAXI (\cref{sec:bench-taxi}) combines real two-language dialogues, human translations of both sides and free research access; others are one-sided (MSLT; \citealp{federmann2016microsoft}), re-enacted (DRAL; \citealp{ward2024dialogs}), machine-mediated (InCroMin; \citealp{cechovic2025corpus}) or from one source language (Fisher--CALLHOME; \citealp{post2013improved}).
