\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\taxi has real speech but synthetic timing and overlap, for want of known public two-language recordings with natural overlap and references; it is small, single-domain, narrowband and close-talk, and ties role to language (\cref{sec:related,sec:bench-taxi}).
\syn has fluent TTS speech, translationese on one side and silver Korean references, so it supports only relative conclusions, checked against \taxi (\cref{tab:validity}).\draftnote{planned; \syn and the turn-end overlap renders are not built yet}
Interruptions, barge-in and backchannels are left for future work.
\model learns from simulated sessions and a privileged teacher (\cref{sec:model-targets}); only the decoupled twin and the same-backbone cascade test whether coupling turn-taking and translation helps (\cref{sec:exp-ablation}).\draftnote{planned; only the streaming ASR backbone is built, and all \model results are pending}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\task requires deciding, from one mono stream, who speaks, which way to translate and when to commit: on \taxi, segmentation and routing account for \tbdtext{} and \tbdtext{} COMET points of the best open pipeline's oracle gap (RQ1), which moves from \tbdtext{} to \tbdtext{} between mediated gaps and turn-end overlap (RQ2), and \model recovers \tbdtext{}\% of it without oracle input at a median \eo of \tbdtext{}\,s (RQ3).
Synthetic and real rankings give $\tau=$ \tbdtext{} (RQ4); real overlapped recordings and more language pairs come next.
