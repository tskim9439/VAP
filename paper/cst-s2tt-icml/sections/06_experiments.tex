\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Systems.}
Offline systems translate each gold turn after it ends, without dialogue history (\cref{tab:baselines,app:baselines}): Qwen3.8-27B (``LLM'') translates the gold transcript (Gold$\to$LLM) or that of Qwen3-ASR-1.7B \citep[ASR$\to$LLM;][]{shi2026qwen3asr}, and Whisper-large-v3 translates into English \citep[Whisper ST;][]{radford2023robust}.
Gold$\to$LLM (MU2) streams gold transcripts to the LLM, which commits at the verified meaning units of \cref{sec:model-targets}, chosen non-causally with the whole turn in view.
The consecutive cascade runs ASR$\to$LLM on segments closed by its own VAD.
Streaming systems are SeamlessStreaming \citep{seamless2023seamless}; a streaming cascade, whose LLM re-translates the growing transcript of the Nemotron~3.5 streaming RNN-T \citep{nvidia2026nemotron35asr} and commits the prefix shared by two consecutive outputs (LocalAgreement-2, LA-2; \citealp{liu2020lowlatency,polak2022cuni}); SeamlessM4T~v2 \citep{seamless2025joint} with AlignAtt \citep[M4T v2 + AlignAtt;][]{papi2023alignatt}; and, outside \cref{tab:main}, StreamSpeech \citep[\deen;][]{zhang2024streamspeech} and, optionally, Hibiki-Zero \citep[\deen, text output;][]{labiausse2026simultaneous} and InfiniSST \citep[\ende;][]{ouyang2025infinisst}.
2$\times$SeamlessStreaming, a naive bidirectional baseline, runs one instance per target language on the whole mix, without routing.
\model runs as M2 (turn-end commits) and M3 (MU2 commits; \cref{sec:model-training}), the latter at a low and a high $\delta_t$.
\draftnote{optional runs: Hibiki-Zero (public weights; only its text output is scored), InfiniSST only if its weights are released, and Canary-1B-v2 \citep{sekoyan2025canary} with AlignAtt}

\paragraph{Wrappers and operating points.}
Single-direction systems run as one instance per target language behind a wrapper, forming a pipeline (\cref{app:baselines}): the oracle wrapper cuts gold turns from the mix and routes each by its gold language; the realistic wrapper segments the stream with Silero VAD \citep{silero2024silero} and routes each segment by spoken language identification restricted to the pair.
Baselines are not fine-tuned; each streaming system has two operating points (one main), chosen on the \syn calibration split by a rule fixed before the test runs.
\draftnote{decision needed: whether the low- or the high-budget point (\cref{app:baselines}) is the main one; until \syn exists, documented defaults stand in for calibrated operating points}

\paragraph{Ranking and the oracle gap.}
We rank systems by COMET \citep{kocmi2021ship}; on \taxi, whose references lack case and punctuation, chrF replaces it if their rankings disagree (\cref{app:scorer}).
Latencies use the ideal clock; \cref{tab:ca-latency} (\cref{app:results}) adds computation-aware latency, also for the ablation controls.
The reference pipeline $P$ covers both directions and has the highest realistic-wrapper COMET on \taxi L0 natural.
Its oracle gap $G=Q_{\mathrm{oracle}}-Q_{\mathrm{real}}$ is its COMET difference between the two wrappers; \model recovers $R=(Q_{\mathrm{ours}}-Q_{\mathrm{real}})/G$ of it, reported with a bootstrap CI only where the CI of $G$ excludes 0.

\Cref{tab:factorial} replaces the oracle components of $P$ singly and jointly; a factor's effect averages the COMET lost by replacing its component over both settings of the other factor, so the effects of segmentation and routing sum to $G$ (\cref{app:results}).

\subsection{Ablations: does the unified design help?}\label{sec:exp-ablation}

\Cref{tab:ablation} changes one design choice of \model (M3) per row, starting from the same M1 checkpoint (\cref{sec:model-training}; rows: \cref{app:results}).
Three controls keep the factorisation of \cref{sec:task}.
The \emph{decoupled twin} fixes M1's speaker tags as hard turn and direction decisions and then translates each segment in a separate pass of the same 1.7B thinker with the same commit policy, so translation cannot revise segmentation or routing.
The same-backbone cascade (M1$\to$LLM) translates M1 transcripts with the LLM under LA-2.
Unlike the baselines, both share \model's training on simulated sessions through M1, so we fixed them, before the test runs, as the tests of coupling turn-taking and translation.
A size-matched cascade (ASR$\to$1.7B LLM) separates design from scale by pairing Qwen3-ASR-1.7B with an LLM of about 1.7B parameters under LA-2.
At matched \laal, \model differs in COMET under L0 natural and L1 by \tbdtext{} and \tbdtext{} from the twin, and by \tbdtext{} and \tbdtext{} from the same-backbone cascade.
